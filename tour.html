    Academic paperConference paper · December 2022

    Forecasting crashes that involve unauthorized vehicles in Bangladesh

    Many small vehicles on Bangladesh's roads are not registered, such as battery-run easy-bikes and locally built nosimons and korimons. They are cheap and popular, but they lack basic safety features. We studied the crashes they were involved in and forecast the next five years.

    • DataPolice-recorded crashes, 2009 – 2015, all of BangladeshProvided by the Accident Research Institute (ARI), BUET
    • MethodARIMA(6,1,7) time-series modelIt learns the pattern of past monthly crashes and projects it forward.
    • FindingsAbout 330 crashes a year expected for 2016 – 2020Hitting a pedestrian was the most common crash type, so pedestrians were the main victims.
    • Published6th International Conference on Advances in Civil Engineering (ICACE 2022)CUET, Chattogram · Huq, Rahman & Shristi

    Undergraduate thesis · Academic paperThesis 2023 · paper March 2024

    Why Dhaka's waterbus failed, and how it could come back

    Rivers form a 110 km loop around Dhaka. In 2010 a waterbus started on 16 km of it, but it shut down within 18 months. My thesis asked why, and what passengers would need to use it.

    • RouteSadarghat to Gabtoli, 16 kmOn the Buriganga and Turag rivers, run by BIWTC from 28 August 2010
    • BoatsTwo boats with 35 seats eachA trip took about 90 minutes, and breakdowns were frequent
    • ResultAbout 27,400 passengers in 17 monthsEvery trip lost money, even when full, and the service stopped within 18 months
    • Survey310 people in 2023At Sadarghat, Showarighat, Narayanganj, Gabtoli and Uttara, plus online
    • BoatsHybrid electric water taxis with 50 seatsAbout 40 km/h: Sadarghat to Gabtoli in 24 to 35 minutes instead of 90
    • Service20 boats on the full 110 km loopTen each way, every 20 minutes at peak and every 40 minutes off-peak, about 2,500 passengers a day

    Published at the 7th International Conference on Civil Engineering for Sustainable Development (ICCESD 2024) · Rahman, Shristi, Shahriar & Rahman

    Research · TeachingGraduate Assistant, ITS Lab, WMU · Aug 2025 – Present

    What does a work zone cost drivers?

    For the Michigan Department of Transportation (MDOT), our lab is evaluating how MDOT estimates the delay and cost that work zones cause road users. Closing a lane cuts capacity, a queue forms, and every vehicle loses time.

    • DataFive sources comparedRITIS, Bluetooth, API feeds, microwave vehicle detection (MVDS) and camera video: speed, volume, travel time, lane closures and work-zone layouts
    • My roleCleaning, processing, visualizing and comparing the dataTo judge which sources suit delay and queue evaluation. I also reviewed vehicle operating cost methods; without enough roadway-condition inputs, the findings supported keeping that cost reviewed but not monetized.

    Winner: a turbo roundabout for a high-crash roundabout

    In a two-lane roundabout, drivers often change lanes inside the circle, and that causes crashes. A turbo roundabout adds raised spiral lane dividers, so drivers pick their lane before entering and cannot change lanes inside.

    • StudiedCrash history, traffic demand, pedestrians and the alternatives
    • DesignedPedestrian crossings, signing and marking, safety and lifecycle cost
    • Benefit–costB/C = 1.12 over 20 yearsThe benefits are larger than the costs
